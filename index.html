Airy₁ Gumbel: Lean Companion

Lean 4 companion to Le Chen and Fei Pu, Gumbel Fluctuations of the Maximum of the Airy₁ Process (arXiv 2611.23658): a kernel-checked proof that the maximum of a constructed Airy₁ process over [0, T], centred and scaled as in the paper, converges to the Gumbel law, under three named inputs.

Verification page Coverage by result Challenge.lean Build and verify

What is proved

The Lean companion proves the normalized Gumbel limit in Theorem 1.1 for a constructed Airy₁ process with continuous paths on the nonnegative time axis. Its headline theorem uses the paper's centering and scaling for real observation lengths and assumes three named inputs: finite-dimensional Airy₁ probability laws, a cubic GOE left-tail bound, and cubic strong mixing. The mesoscopic step uses a direct two-time Fredholm determinant estimate and a high-level upcrossing bound, replacing the paper's association and covariance argument. The verification claim covers this conditional theorem and the listed component results.

The headline is Airy1Gumbel.gumbel_limit_continuum_real_normalized_airy1_final₂:

theorem gumbel_limit_continuum_real_normalized_airy1_final₂ (h : Airy1FddPositive)
    (hT : GOELeftTail) (M : CubicStrongMixing (airy1SRP h hT)) (z : ℝ) :
    Tendsto (fun T : ℝ => (airy1Law h).real {f | ∀ x ∈ Set.Icc (0 : ℝ) T,
      (airy1ContR h x f - gumbelAR T) / gumbelBR T ≤ z})
      atTop (𝓝 (Real.exp (-Real.exp (-z))))

Here airy1Law h is the law on ℝ → ℝ built by Kolmogorov extension from the finite-dimensional Airy₁ laws, airy1ContR h is its version with continuous paths on [0, ∞), and gumbelAR, gumbelBR are the centering and scaling of the paper's equations (1.1)–(1.2) for real T. The three hypotheses are Lean structures stated in Airy1Gumbel/FddRecord.lean, Airy1Gumbel/GOELeftTail.lean and Airy1Gumbel/ExternalInputs.lean:

Input What it asserts Source
Airy1FddPositive for every finite set of times, a probability measure whose box distribution function is the Airy₁ Fredholm-determinant formula AiryFdd.airy1FinCdf Quastel–Remenik, Theorem 1; Borodin–Ferrari–Prähofer (2007)
GOELeftTail a cubic left-tail bound F(−M) ≤ C e^{−cM³} (M ≥ 0) for the one-point law Baik–Buckingham–DiFranco (2008), Section 1.2, with the Ferrari–Spohn GOE identity
CubicStrongMixing strong mixing of the process between past and future σ-algebras at rate exp(−c d³) in the gap d Chen–Pu, Proposition 3.1 (arXiv 2607.17113)

Proved in Lean on the way, among others: the centering and normalization lemmas; the block-maxima (blocking) assembly; the sharp one-block tail and the touching and separated pair bounds from the Quastel–Remenik block laws, which are themselves derived for the constructed process from a dyadic Brownian hitting theorem; the two-time Fredholm determinant bound and the high-level upcrossing bound used in the mesoscopic step. The nested package contrib/ holds the analytic infrastructure: the Gumbel law, Airy functions and their asymptotics, the Mills ratio, Hilbert–Schmidt and trace-class operators, Fredholm determinants, and the Airy₁ finite-dimensional and two-time estimates. Newman's covariance inequality (newman_lemma3) is also proved; the headline does not use it, and the earlier association-route headline and Lemma 4.2 (since v0.5.0) do.

Not proved here.

  • The three inputs above are assumptions of the headline theorem, not theorems. GOELeftTail is an extra explicit input relative to the paper, which starts from an existing continuous Airy₁ process.

  • The paper proves its mesoscopic step through association (Pu 2025), Newman's inequality and the Basu–Busani–Ferrari covariance asymptotic. The headline's Lean proof takes a different route for that step (a two-time Fredholm determinant bound), so it does not check the paper's Proposition 4.3 as stated (arbitrary level M and cutoff R(M)); only the specialisation used in Section 5.2 is proved.

  • Lemma 4.2 (the threshold-sensitive Newman bound, eq. (4.3)) is proved for the constructed process (since v0.5.0) under the standing inputs Airy1FddPositive and GOELeftTail and the association input ProcessAssociated: the association of the Airy₁ process (Pu 2025, Theorem 1.2), which is assumed, not proved, here. The headline _final₂ still uses exactly its three inputs and does not use ProcessAssociated.

  • Lemma 4.1 (the block modulus, eq. (4.1)) is proved for the constructed process (since v0.4.0) under Airy1FddPositive, GOELeftTail and a fourth named input, NonnegativeWindowModulusEstimate: the transferred local oscillation bound (4.2) of arXiv:2611.23658v1 on windows inside [0, ∞), which the paper derives from Basu–Bhattacharjee (2024) and which is assumed, not proved, here. The headline _final₂ still uses exactly its three inputs and does not use the new one.

  • Proposition 1.2 is proved for the constructed process for every T > 0 (since v0.2.0), under Airy1FddPositive and GOELeftTail; like the headline, it is conditional on those inputs.

  • Lemma 3.1 is proved for the constructed process for two intervals of any equal length T > 0 (since v0.3.0), under Airy1FddPositive and GOELeftTail. What is formalized is the trace-class form (3.3) of Remark 3.2, det(I − K̃∪) with the conjugated kernel. The determinant displayed in (3.1) has no separate Lean definition: the paper reads it through (3.3), and that reading is the paper's, not a Lean theorem.

  • Remark 5.3 (the Ferrari–Liu derivation) is not covered.

  • This repository does not certify the whole manuscript: it checks the main theorem along the route described above and the component results listed in claims.yaml and contrib/claims.yaml.

Since v0.5.0, Lemma 4.2 (the threshold-sensitive Newman bound, eq. (4.3)) is proved for the constructed continuous process under the standing inputs Airy1FddPositive and GOELeftTail and the association input Airy1Gumbel.ProcessAssociated: the association of the Airy₁ process (Pu 2025, Theorem 1.2), in the form that every finite-dimensional marginal gives nonnegative covariance to bounded, monotone, measurable test functions. ProcessAssociated is an existing named input, assumed, not proved, here; until this release only the earlier association-route headline _final (below) and its intermediate forms used it. GOELeftTail is not a hypothesis of the paper's Lemma 4.2; it is a standing input of every process-level result here because the process is constructed. Under these inputs, Airy1Gumbel.tail_sensitive_newman_airy1 states that there are C > 0 and u₀ such that, for every u ≥ u₀ and every lag d ≥ 0, ℙ{Ã(0) > u, Ã(d) > u} ≤ C ℙ{Ã(0) > u}² + C u Cov(Ã(0), Ã(d)); the constants do not depend on d. The covariance is a genuine one: both marginals are proved square-integrable (memLp_two_airy1ContR), so it is not the value 0 that Lean assigns off L². The one-point tail ratio ℙ{Ã(0) > u − u^{−1/2}} ≤ K ℙ{Ã(0) > u}, which the paper takes from the density bound (4.4)–(4.5), is proved here from the GOE Fredholm determinant (one_point_tail_shift_le_airy1, through the sharp singleton tail goe_singleton_right_tail_sub_profile_isLittleO), so this release adds no new named input. The headline _final₂ still uses exactly its three inputs and does not use ProcessAssociated, and no existing statement changed. The six new claims are internal: Lean's kernel checked their proofs, but the comparator does not compare their statements.

Since v0.4.0, Lemma 4.1 (the block modulus, eq. (4.1)) is proved for the constructed process under Airy1FddPositive, GOELeftTail and a fourth named input, Airy1Gumbel.NonnegativeWindowModulusEstimate: the transferred local oscillation bound (4.2) of arXiv:2611.23658v1 on windows inside [0, ∞), which the paper derives from Basu–Bhattacharjee (2024, arXiv 2406.11826, Lemma 5.1 with Theorem 1.4) and which is assumed, not proved, here. The input says that there are C₀, c₀ > 0 such that, for all sufficiently large ℓ, every radius 0 < s ≤ 1 and every centre c ≥ s, ℙ{|Ã(t₁) − Ã(t₂)| > C₀ ℓ √s for some t₁, t₂ ∈ [c − s, c + s]} ≤ C₀ e^{−c₀ ℓ}; it is not a verbatim Basu–Bhattacharjee statement. Under these inputs, Airy1Gumbel.block_modulus_airy1 states that there are C, c, h₀ > 0 such that, for 0 < δ ≤ h₀, ℙ{|Ã(x) − Ã(y)| > 1 for some x, y ∈ [0, 1] with |x − y| ≤ 2δ} ≤ C δ⁻¹ e^{−c δ^{−1/2}}. Two audit theorems check that the input is neither vacuous nor trivially true: the zero process satisfies it (nonnegativeWindowModulusEstimate_const) and the process t² violates it (isEmpty_nonnegativeWindowModulusEstimate_sq). This is the first release that adds a named input. The headline _final₂ still uses exactly its three inputs and does not use the new one (its mesoscopic step goes through a high-level upcrossing bound), and no existing statement changed. The new claims are internal: Lean's kernel checked their proofs, but the comparator does not compare their statements.

Since v0.3.0, Lemma 3.1 (the two-interval determinant formula) is proved for the constructed process for every equal length T > 0, under Airy1FddPositive and GOELeftTail: Airy1Gumbel.airy1Law_two_blocks_le_eq_fredholmDet_of_length states that, for T > 0, r > 0, M > 0, x ≥ 0 and any weight a ≥ 2T + r + 1, ℙ{Ã ≤ M on [x, x+T] and on [x+T+r, x+2T+r]} = det(I − K̃∪), where K̃∪ = U_a K∪ U_a⁻¹ is the conjugated kernel of (3.2)–(3.3) in Remark 3.2. The paper's case is x = 0, a = √(2M). Versions 0.1.0 and 0.2.0 had this law for two unit intervals only; that statement is unchanged and is now the case T = 1.

Since v0.2.0, Proposition 1.2 (the two-term expansion of the tail of the maximum over a fixed interval) is proved for the constructed process for every length T > 0, under Airy1FddPositive and GOELeftTail: Airy1Gumbel.airy1Law_interval_tail_sub_traceLead_isLittleO states that ℙ{∃ t ∈ [0, T], Ã(t) > M} − (T·ρ(M) + ρ(M)/(4M)) = o(ρ(M)/M) as M → ∞, where Ã is the continuous version airy1ContR h. Version 0.1.0 had this expansion for the Fredholm determinant for every T > 0, and for the process only for T ≥ 1. See CHANGELOG.md.

An earlier headline, gumbel_limit_continuum_real_normalized_airy1_final (Airy1Gumbel/FinalHeadline.lean), follows the paper's association route and therefore also assumes the covariance-decay and association records CubicCovarianceDecay and ProcessAssociated.
