Airy₁ Gumbel: Lean Companion

Lean 4 companion to Le Chen and Fei Pu, Gumbel Fluctuations of the Maximum of the Airy₁ Process (arXiv 2611.23658): a kernel-checked proof that the maximum of a constructed Airy₁ process over [0, T], centred and scaled as in the paper, converges to the Gumbel law, under three named inputs. Since v0.9.0 the first input, Airy1FddPositive, is proved under the second, GOELeftTail, so the same limit also holds under two named inputs (GOELeftTail and CubicStrongMixing).

What is proved

The Lean companion proves the normalized Gumbel limit in Theorem 1.1 for a constructed Airy₁ process with continuous paths on the nonnegative time axis. Its headline theorem uses the paper's centering and scaling for real observation lengths and assumes three named inputs: finite-dimensional Airy₁ probability laws, a cubic GOE left-tail bound, and cubic strong mixing. The mesoscopic step uses a direct two-time Fredholm determinant estimate and a high-level upcrossing bound, replacing the paper's association and covariance argument. The verification claim covers this conditional theorem and the listed component results.

The headline is Airy1Gumbel.gumbel_limit_continuum_real_normalized_airy1_final₂:

theorem gumbel_limit_continuum_real_normalized_airy1_final₂ (h : Airy1FddPositive)
    (hT : GOELeftTail) (M : CubicStrongMixing (airy1SRP h hT)) (z : ℝ) :
    Tendsto (fun T : ℝ => (airy1Law h).real {f | ∀ x ∈ Set.Icc (0 : ℝ) T,
      (airy1ContR h x f - gumbelAR T) / gumbelBR T ≤ z})
      atTop (𝓝 (Real.exp (-Real.exp (-z))))

Here airy1Law h is the law on ℝ → ℝ built by Kolmogorov extension from the finite-dimensional Airy₁ laws, airy1ContR h is its version with continuous paths on [0, ∞), and gumbelAR, gumbelBR are the centering and scaling of the paper's equations (1.1)–(1.2) for real T. The three hypotheses are Lean structures stated in Airy1Gumbel/FddRecord.lean, Airy1Gumbel/GOELeftTail.lean and Airy1Gumbel/ExternalInputs.lean:

Input What it asserts Source
Airy1FddPositive for every finite set of times, a probability measure whose box distribution function is the Airy₁ Fredholm-determinant formula AiryFdd.airy1FinCdf Quastel–Remenik, Theorem 1; Borodin–Ferrari–Prähofer (2007)
GOELeftTail a cubic left-tail bound F(−M) ≤ C e^{−cM³} (M ≥ 0) for the one-point law Baik–Buckingham–DiFranco (2008), Section 1.2, with the Ferrari–Spohn GOE identity
CubicStrongMixing strong mixing of the process between past and future σ-algebras at rate exp(−c d³) in the gap d Chen–Pu, Proposition 3.1 (arXiv 2607.17113)

Proved in Lean on the way, among others: the centering and normalization lemmas; the block-maxima (blocking) assembly; the sharp one-block tail and the touching and separated pair bounds from the Quastel–Remenik block laws, which are themselves derived for the constructed process from a dyadic Brownian hitting theorem; the two-time Fredholm determinant bound and the high-level upcrossing bound used in the mesoscopic step. The nested package contrib/ holds the analytic infrastructure: the Gumbel law, Airy functions and their asymptotics, the Mills ratio, Hilbert–Schmidt and trace-class operators, Fredholm determinants, and the Airy₁ finite-dimensional and two-time estimates. Newman's covariance inequality (newman_lemma3) is also proved; the headline does not use it, and the earlier association-route headline, Lemma 4.2 (since v0.5.0) and Proposition 4.3 (since v0.6.0) do.

Not proved here.

  • The three inputs above are assumptions of the headline theorem, not theorems. GOELeftTail is an extra explicit input relative to the paper, which starts from an existing continuous Airy₁ process. Since v0.9.0 the first input, Airy1FddPositive, is proved under the second (airy1FddPositive_of_goeLeftTail), not unconditionally; _final₂_no_fdd_input has the headline's conclusion under GOELeftTail and CubicStrongMixing only, and those two remain assumptions.

  • The paper proves its mesoscopic step through association (Pu 2025), Newman's inequality and the Basu–Busani–Ferrari covariance asymptotic. The headline's Lean proof takes a different route for that step (a two-time Fredholm determinant bound) and uses only the specialisation of Proposition 4.3 needed in Section 5.2, which it proves along that route. Proposition 4.3 as stated (eq. (4.6), every χ ∈ ((1/2)^{1/3}, 1) and every cutoff R(M) = O(√M)) is proved separately (since v0.6.0), along the paper's route, under the standing inputs Airy1FddPositive and GOELeftTail and three named inputs, each assumed, not proved, here: NonnegativeWindowModulusEstimate, ProcessAssociated and CubicCovarianceDecay. The headline _final₂ uses none of these three.

  • Lemma 4.2 (the threshold-sensitive Newman bound, eq. (4.3)) is proved for the constructed process (since v0.5.0) under the standing inputs Airy1FddPositive and GOELeftTail and the association input ProcessAssociated: the association of the Airy₁ process (Pu 2025, Theorem 1.2), which is assumed, not proved, here. The headline _final₂ still uses exactly its three inputs and does not use ProcessAssociated.

  • Lemma 4.1 (the block modulus, eq. (4.1)) is proved for the constructed process (since v0.4.0) under Airy1FddPositive, GOELeftTail and a fourth named input, NonnegativeWindowModulusEstimate: the transferred local oscillation bound (4.2) of arXiv:2611.23658v1 on windows inside [0, ∞), which the paper derives from Basu–Bhattacharjee (2024) and which is assumed, not proved, here. The headline _final₂ still uses exactly its three inputs and does not use the new one.

  • Proposition 1.2 is proved for the constructed process for every T > 0 (since v0.2.0), under Airy1FddPositive and GOELeftTail; like the headline, it is conditional on those inputs.

  • Lemma 3.1 is proved for the constructed process for two intervals of any equal length T > 0 (since v0.3.0), under Airy1FddPositive and GOELeftTail. What is formalized is the trace-class form (3.3) of Remark 3.2, det(I − K̃∪) with the conjugated kernel. The determinant displayed in (3.1) has no separate Lean definition: the paper reads it through (3.3), and that reading is the paper's, not a Lean theorem.

  • Remark 5.3 (the Gumbel limit derived from the Ferrari–Liu persistence estimates) is proved for the constructed process (since v0.8.0), along integer lengths as the remark states, under the standing inputs Airy1FddPositive and GOELeftTail and a new named input, FerrariLiuPersistence: the finite-length persistence expansion of Ferrari–Liu (2024), which is assumed, not proved, here and is used only by Remark 5.3. The headline _final₂ does not use it.

  • Airy1FddPositive is proved under GOELeftTail (since v0.9.0), not unconditionally. The original theorems, the headline _final₂ and the statements the comparator checks among them, still take it as a hypothesis, unchanged; the _no_fdd_input forms do not take it. Since v0.9.0 the comparator also checks airy1FddPositive_of_goeLeftTail and _final₂_no_fdd_input; the other _no_fdd_input forms are built, not compared.

  • Lemmas 3.3–3.8 (the intermediate lemmas of the paper's proof of Proposition 1.4) are proved in the contrib package, 3.6 and 3.7 as stated, 3.3, 3.4 and 3.5 in a different form and 3.8 in a weaker form (Hilbert–Schmidt norms, not trace norms). Lemmas B.1–B.6 of Appendix B are proved in the contrib library as stated (B.4 and B.6 on a larger parameter range), with no named input. All twelve are shown as outside in coverage.yaml only because its rows can name main-package claims alone; each row gives the declarations.

  • This repository does not certify the whole manuscript: it checks the main theorem along the route described above and the component results listed in claims.yaml and contrib/claims.yaml.

Since v0.9.0, Airy1FddPositive is proved under GOELeftTail, and the headline holds under two named inputs. Airy1Gumbel.airy1FddPositive_of_goeLeftTail (hT : GOELeftTail) : Airy1FddPositive constructs, for every finite set of times, a probability law whose box distribution function is AiryFdd.airy1FinCdf: the nested package formalizes flat discrete TASEP (sequential update) and its finite-window law, writes its gap probabilities as Fredholm series of the Borodin–Ferrari–Prähofer kernel, and proves that the rescaled distribution functions converge to the extended Airy₁ determinants (AiryFdd.tendsto_flatTasepCdf_extendedSeries, AiryFdd.airyExtendedSeries_eq_airy1FinCdf, in contrib/Airy1GumbelContrib/FddMeasure/). The limits inherit nonnegativity, monotonicity and nonnegative rectangle increments, and the v0.8.0 reduction airy1FddPositive_of_rectangle turns them into the measures, with GOELeftTail for the behaviour at −∞. It is proved under GOELeftTail, not unconditionally. The new headline is Airy1Gumbel.gumbel_limit_continuum_real_normalized_airy1_final₂_no_fdd_input:

theorem gumbel_limit_continuum_real_normalized_airy1_final₂_no_fdd_input (hT : GOELeftTail)
    (M : CubicStrongMixing (airy1SRP (airy1FddPositive_of_goeLeftTail hT) hT)) (z : ℝ) :
    Tendsto (fun T : ℝ => (airy1Law (airy1FddPositive_of_goeLeftTail hT)).real {f | ∀ x ∈ Set.Icc (0 : ℝ) T,
      (airy1ContR (airy1FddPositive_of_goeLeftTail hT) x f - gumbelAR T) / gumbelBR T ≤ z})
      atTop (𝓝 (Real.exp (-Real.exp (-z))))

It is _final₂ at h := airy1FddPositive_of_goeLeftTail hT: the same conclusion under GOELeftTail and CubicStrongMixing only. No input is added, and _final₂ and every earlier statement are unchanged; the originals still take Airy1FddPositive as a hypothesis. Airy1Gumbel/FddFromTasepCorollaries.lean has 175 such _no_fdd_input forms of earlier results, each its original with the hypothesis Airy1FddPositive supplied by airy1FddPositive_of_goeLeftTail (taking GOELeftTail where the original did not); every other hypothesis of the original, among them the named inputs of Lemmas 4.1, 4.2, Proposition 4.3 and Remark 5.3, is kept. Of the 44 new claims of the main package, two are compared (since v0.9.0 the comparator checks airy1FddPositive_of_goeLeftTail and _final₂_no_fdd_input against statements of Challenge.lean; see below). The other 42 and the 9 new claims of the contrib package are internal: Lean's kernel checked their proofs, but the comparator does not compare their statements.

Since v0.8.0, Remark 5.3 (the Gumbel limit derived from the Ferrari–Liu persistence estimates) is proved for the constructed continuous process, along integer lengths as the remark states. Airy1Gumbel.gumbel_limit_continuum_airy1_ferrariLiu states that, for every real z, ℙ{Ã(x) ≤ a_N + b_N z for all x ∈ [0, N]} → exp(−e^{−z}) as N → ∞ through the integers, with the centering and scaling a_N, b_N of (1.1)–(1.2) (gumbelU N z). Its hypotheses are the standing inputs Airy1FddPositive and GOELeftTail and a new named input, Airy1Gumbel.FerrariLiuPersistence: the finite-length persistence expansion of Ferrari–Liu (2024, Theorem 1.1, with the bounds of their Proposition 2.5 and Section 2.4), which is assumed, not proved, here. For the continuous version on [0, ∞) it says that, for every level c ≥ 3/2 and length L ≥ 1, ℙ{Ã(t) ≤ c for all t ∈ [0, L]} = exp(−L κ(c) − S(c) + E(c, L)), where κ(c) = Σ_{n ≥ 1} −2 n^{−5/3} Ai′(2 n^{2/3} c) is the Ferrari–Liu persistence rate, |S(c)| ≤ −6 log(1 − 2β(c)) and |E(c, L)| ≤ 2 e^{−4L³/3} + Σ_{n ≥ 2} 7^{n+1} β(c)ⁿ / n · e^{−4L³/(3n²)}, with the explicit two-branch parameter β(c) of their Section 2.4 (ferrariLiuBeta). What is assumed includes Ferrari–Liu's identity log det(1 − K) = −Σ_n Tr(Kⁿ)/n; the input's docstring also records the sign convention of the rate and the coefficient 2 in the degree-one error. Proved here: the bound on β(c), the summation of the error series, the vanishing of the offset S, and the asymptotic κ(c) ~ ρ(c) that turns the expansion into the Gumbel limit (ferrariLiuKappa_div_rhoM_tendsto). This is the second release that adds a named input (the first was v0.4.0). FerrariLiuPersistence is used only by Remark 5.3 (through its lemma ferrariLiu_remainders); the headline _final₂ does not use it and still uses exactly its three inputs, and no existing statement changed. The 25 new claims of this route are internal: Lean's kernel checked their proofs, but the comparator does not compare their statements.

Also since v0.8.0, a first step towards proving Airy1FddPositive, which is still an input. The nested package proves a finite-box measure constructor, AiryFdd.exists_measure_of_box_cdf: a continuous function F on ℝ^ι (ι finite) that is nonnegative, monotone, gives every box (a, b] a nonnegative alternating corner sum (boxCdfIncrement F a b ≥ 0), tends to 0 when one coordinate tends to −∞ and to 1 along the diagonal x = (r, …, r), r → +∞, is the distribution function x ↦ μ(∏ᵢ (−∞, xᵢ]) of a probability measure μ. With it, Airy1Gumbel.airy1FddPositive_of_rectangle derives Airy1FddPositive from GOELeftTail and three properties of the Fredholm-determinant formulas AiryFdd.airy1FinCdf I, for every finite set of times I: nonnegativity, monotonicity and nonnegative rectangle increments. In v0.8.0 those three properties were hypotheses of the theorem; v0.9.0 proves them (see above). The new claims (one in each package) are internal.

Since v0.6.0, Proposition 4.3 (the mesoscopic sum, eq. (4.6)) is proved as a standalone result for the constructed continuous process, along the paper's route. Write Y_j for the maximum of Ã over [j, j + 1] (airy1BlockMax h j) and ρ(M) for the one-block tail scale of (1.3) (AiryConj.rhoM). Airy1Gumbel.mesoscopic_sum_airy1 states that, for every χ with (1/2)^{1/3} < χ < 1 and every cutoff R : ℝ → ℝ with R(M) = O(√M), Σ_{⌈χ√(2M)⌉ ≤ j ≤ ⌊R(M)⌋} ℙ{Y_0 > M, Y_j > M} = o(ρ(M)) as M → ∞ through the reals (an empty range gives the sum 0). Its five hypotheses are the standing inputs Airy1FddPositive and GOELeftTail and three named inputs, each assumed, not proved, here:

  • NonnegativeWindowModulusEstimate: the transferred local oscillation bound (4.2) on windows inside [0, ∞) (Basu–Bhattacharjee 2024); already the input of Lemma 4.1.
  • ProcessAssociated: the association of the Airy₁ process (Pu 2025, Theorem 1.2); already the input of Lemma 4.2.
  • CubicCovarianceDecay: the covariance asymptotic of the Airy₁ process (Basu–Busani–Ferrari 2023, Theorem 1.1), in the form of its upper bound Cov(Ã(0), Ã(d)) ≤ e^{c d² − (4/3) d³} for d > 1; until this release only the earlier association-route headline _final (below) and its intermediate forms used it.

The proof uses the first through Lemma 4.1 (the block modulus on [0, 1], carried to every block [j, j + 1] by shift invariance of the law), the second through Lemma 4.2, and the third only through its proved corollary, the cubic covariance bound Cov(Ã(0), Ã(d)) ≤ e^{−θd³} for every θ < 4/3 and large d (CubicCovarianceDecay.theta_bound). The block-to-grid cover (4.10) and the one-point bound (4.11) at level M − 1 are proved (block_pair_grid_cover_airy1, one_point_tail_minus_one_airy1). This release adds no new named input. The headline _final₂ still uses exactly its three inputs and none of these three, and no existing statement changed. The five new claims are internal: Lean's kernel checked their proofs, but the comparator does not compare their statements.

Since v0.5.0, Lemma 4.2 (the threshold-sensitive Newman bound, eq. (4.3)) is proved for the constructed continuous process under the standing inputs Airy1FddPositive and GOELeftTail and the association input Airy1Gumbel.ProcessAssociated: the association of the Airy₁ process (Pu 2025, Theorem 1.2), in the form that every finite-dimensional marginal gives nonnegative covariance to bounded, monotone, measurable test functions. ProcessAssociated is an existing named input, assumed, not proved, here; until this release only the earlier association-route headline _final (below) and its intermediate forms used it. GOELeftTail is not a hypothesis of the paper's Lemma 4.2; it is a standing input of every process-level result here because the process is constructed. Under these inputs, Airy1Gumbel.tail_sensitive_newman_airy1 states that there are C > 0 and u₀ such that, for every u ≥ u₀ and every lag d ≥ 0, ℙ{Ã(0) > u, Ã(d) > u} ≤ C ℙ{Ã(0) > u}² + C u Cov(Ã(0), Ã(d)); the constants do not depend on d. The covariance is a genuine one: both marginals are proved square-integrable (memLp_two_airy1ContR), so it is not the value 0 that Lean assigns off L². The one-point tail ratio ℙ{Ã(0) > u − u^{−1/2}} ≤ K ℙ{Ã(0) > u}, which the paper takes from the density bound (4.4)–(4.5), is proved here from the GOE Fredholm determinant (one_point_tail_shift_le_airy1, through the sharp singleton tail goe_singleton_right_tail_sub_profile_isLittleO), so this release adds no new named input. The headline _final₂ still uses exactly its three inputs and does not use ProcessAssociated, and no existing statement changed. The six new claims are internal: Lean's kernel checked their proofs, but the comparator does not compare their statements.

Since v0.4.0, Lemma 4.1 (the block modulus, eq. (4.1)) is proved for the constructed process under Airy1FddPositive, GOELeftTail and a fourth named input, Airy1Gumbel.NonnegativeWindowModulusEstimate: the transferred local oscillation bound (4.2) of arXiv:2611.23658v1 on windows inside [0, ∞), which the paper derives from Basu–Bhattacharjee (2024, arXiv 2406.11826, Lemma 5.1 with Theorem 1.4) and which is assumed, not proved, here. The input says that there are C₀, c₀ > 0 such that, for all sufficiently large ℓ, every radius 0 < s ≤ 1 and every centre c ≥ s, ℙ{|Ã(t₁) − Ã(t₂)| > C₀ ℓ √s for some t₁, t₂ ∈ [c − s, c + s]} ≤ C₀ e^{−c₀ ℓ}; it is not a verbatim Basu–Bhattacharjee statement. Under these inputs, Airy1Gumbel.block_modulus_airy1 states that there are C, c, h₀ > 0 such that, for 0 < δ ≤ h₀, ℙ{|Ã(x) − Ã(y)| > 1 for some x, y ∈ [0, 1] with |x − y| ≤ 2δ} ≤ C δ⁻¹ e^{−c δ^{−1/2}}. Two audit theorems check that the input is neither vacuous nor trivially true: the zero process satisfies it (nonnegativeWindowModulusEstimate_const) and the process t² violates it (isEmpty_nonnegativeWindowModulusEstimate_sq). This is the first release that adds a named input. The headline _final₂ still uses exactly its three inputs and does not use the new one (its mesoscopic step goes through a high-level upcrossing bound), and no existing statement changed. The new claims are internal: Lean's kernel checked their proofs, but the comparator does not compare their statements.

Since v0.3.0, Lemma 3.1 (the two-interval determinant formula) is proved for the constructed process for every equal length T > 0, under Airy1FddPositive and GOELeftTail: Airy1Gumbel.airy1Law_two_blocks_le_eq_fredholmDet_of_length states that, for T > 0, r > 0, M > 0, x ≥ 0 and any weight a ≥ 2T + r + 1, ℙ{Ã ≤ M on [x, x+T] and on [x+T+r, x+2T+r]} = det(I − K̃∪), where K̃∪ = U_a K∪ U_a⁻¹ is the conjugated kernel of (3.2)–(3.3) in Remark 3.2. The paper's case is x = 0, a = √(2M). Versions 0.1.0 and 0.2.0 had this law for two unit intervals only; that statement is unchanged and is now the case T = 1.

Since v0.2.0, Proposition 1.2 (the two-term expansion of the tail of the maximum over a fixed interval) is proved for the constructed process for every length T > 0, under Airy1FddPositive and GOELeftTail: Airy1Gumbel.airy1Law_interval_tail_sub_traceLead_isLittleO states that ℙ{∃ t ∈ [0, T], Ã(t) > M} − (T·ρ(M) + ρ(M)/(4M)) = o(ρ(M)/M) as M → ∞, where Ã is the continuous version airy1ContR h. Version 0.1.0 had this expansion for the Fredholm determinant for every T > 0, and for the process only for T ≥ 1. See CHANGELOG.md.

An earlier headline, gumbel_limit_continuum_real_normalized_airy1_final (Airy1Gumbel/FinalHeadline.lean), follows the paper's association route and therefore also assumes the covariance-decay and association records CubicCovarianceDecay and ProcessAssociated.
